What is an old mine cut diamond?

November 10, 2026  ·  By Nelly Mendoza-Mendoza

The short answer: an old mine cut is an early form of the brilliant cut, cut by hand and popular from the 1700s through the late 1800s. It has a softly squared, cushion-shaped outline, a high crown, a small table, a deep pavilion and a large open culet, which you can often see as a small circle when you look down through the top of the stone.

Where the name comes from

The name is usually traced to the "old mines" of India and Brazil, which supplied most diamonds before the large South African finds of the late 1800s.

How an old mine cut looks

Modern round brilliants are cut for many small, sharp sparkles. An old mine cut gives broader, softer flashes of light, often described as candlelight sparkle. Each stone was cut by eye, so no two are exactly alike.

Old mine cut, old European cut and modern cushion cuts

  • Old European cut: a later, round antique cut, also with a large culet, common from the late 1800s into the 1930s.
  • Modern cushion cut: a squared shape cut with today's machinery, usually with a small or pointed culet and a different facet pattern.
  • "Antique cushion" or new old mine cut: some cutters today cut new diamonds in the old style. They can be beautiful, but they are not antique.

What to ask before you buy

  • Is the diamond antique, or newly cut in the old style?
  • What are its carat weight, color and clarity, and is there a grading report?
  • Has it been recut? Antique stones are sometimes recut to look more modern.

Because more of an old mine cut's weight sits below the girdle, it can look smaller face-up than a modern round of the same carat weight. Judge the stone by how it looks, not by its weight alone.

Questions

Are old mine cut diamonds more valuable than modern diamonds?
Not automatically. Value depends on carat, color, clarity and the quality of the cut, plus demand for antique cuts. Compare stones one by one.

Why does an old mine cut diamond have a hole in the middle?
That circle is the culet, a small flat facet at the bottom of the stone. Old mine cuts have a large culet, so you can often see it through the top.

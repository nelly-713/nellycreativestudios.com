Studio Life

5 Years in New York

November 15, 2026  ·  By Nelly Mendoza-Mendoza

Time has truly flown by — when I first moved to New York, I was still in my 20s and had the ambition to make it big. I moved here with nothing more than a suitcase and a bag full of dreams. I felt a bit lost when I first moved here in 2021. The COVID times were still going as strong as ever and I was unsure about what the future would hold, but little by little I built a home for myself

Something I had not realized until now is how difficult the pandemic days were. I had just graduated and I had no clue how I would figure out my crazy career in a time when everyone was just worried about keeping themselves safe. I had not realized how difficult those first two years post-grad were until now, but I am so grateful I pushed through. On top of the uncertainty of going into a creative career, I was trying to start a business from scratch and navigating an “extenuating circumstances” environment

Looking back at my time in NYC, my time on 53rd St in Midtown East in my 3rd floor walkup, my time on 31st between Park and Lex also in Midtown in my “Penthouse 4F” apartment and now in the West Village, life has never stopped surprising me. I became a downtown girlie and yes I do pilates! I learned before it became “cool” though - when I first moved here in 2021 and I was trying to find community. I didn’t make any friends through it but I fell in love with pilates

I have learned that it is good to not be everyone’s cup of tea, to be kind to strangers, to actively practice kindness, to be patient with myself and to always be working. And by that I mean going on walks, painting, taking the time to smell the flowers and the time to take care of myself so I can be the most creative I can be. Work does not have to be a negative word

During those five years, I also got a job at Tiffany’s in the innovation studio making all the new cool pieces, tried on $10+ million pieces, modeled for the With Love Since 1837 jewelry campaign and left after almost four years. I ran three NYC marathons back to back, spent weeks in California learning gouache painting, got my GIA graduate gemologist degree, started going to all the big jewelry trade shows including Tucson (February) and Las Vegas (June), had my first assistant, made my first five-figure sale and the list goes on and on

And there is nothing I would change — actually, maybe one thing: realizing earlier that everything would work out, and that all the days I spent worrying, I could have spent working on my business

I love New York

What is gold granulation? A goldsmith's guide

November 10, 2026  ·  By Nelly Mendoza-Mendoza

The short answer: gold granulation is a goldsmithing technique in which tiny spheres of gold, called granules, are fused onto a gold surface to form patterns, without filling the joins with visible solder. It is one of the oldest decorative techniques in jewelry. Examples survive from the Royal Tombs of Ur in Mesopotamia, from around 2500 BCE, and the Etruscans of ancient Italy took it to an extraordinary fineness in the 7th and 6th centuries BCE.

How granulation is made

Every stage is done by hand.

  • Alloying. 24 karat gold is alloyed down to 22 karat with a little copper and silver, for strength without losing the color of high-karat gold.
  • Making the granules. A thin sheet of the alloy is cut into tiny squares, which are heated on a tilted charcoal block until each melts into a sphere and rolls into a bowl of water.
  • Placing them. Each granule is set by hand with fine tweezers onto a base of the same alloy, and coated with a little copper salt solution.
  • Fusing. The piece is heated slowly until it glows red, close to its melting point. The copper lowers the melting point just where each granule touches the base, so the granules fuse there and nowhere else. A few seconds too long and the whole design melts.
  • Inspecting. Every granule is checked, and the piece is reheated with great care if any have not fused.

I walk through each step in detail in how the Dartmouth D Gold Medallion is made.

Why 22 karat gold

22 karat gold is 91.7% pure gold. It fuses cleanly at the high heat granulation needs, and its warm, saturated color shows the texture. Every part of a piece, the granules and the base, has to be the same alloy, because the whole piece is heated close to its melting point and any part that melts sooner ruins the design.

How to tell real granulation from an imitation

Much "granulated" jewelry is cast in one piece from a mold. Cast work usually has softer, rounded joins and filled gaps between the beads. True granulation shows separate spheres that touch at small points, with fine shadows between them, and slight natural variation from one granule to the next.

Caring for granulated jewelry

Well-fused granules are permanently bonded, but 22k gold is softer than 14k or 18k. Store each piece separately, avoid hard knocks, and clean it with mild soap, warm water and a soft brush. Ask your jeweler before using an ultrasonic cleaner.

Questions

Is granulation the same as milgrain?
No. Milgrain is a row of tiny beads pressed into the edge of metal with a tool. Granulation uses separate spheres of gold, each fused to the surface.

Is granulated jewelry durable?
Yes. Properly fused granules are permanently bonded to the piece. 22k gold is softer than lower karats, so store it separately and avoid hard knocks.
